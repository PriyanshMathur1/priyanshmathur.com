about

the short version of a long detour

Placement season at BIT Mesra arrived in 2020 and happened to other people. So I built online stores off YouTube. Then a second. Then seven at once, because I couldn't tell which would work. I learned ads the same way and spent everything waiting for them to work. The money ran out before the sales showed up.

So I learned SEO on my own dead stores, cold-messaged the owner of an SEO agency in Australia, and talked my way in with no experience and no portfolio. I started as the intern and went on to lead the team. Since then I've done the same job with more at stake each time: find where intent already sits, then build the thing that meets it there.

I've never been able to separate "is this good?" from "will anyone ever find it?" To me those are one question. A run of dead stores is why.

the decision I'd defend hardestscoping the MVP for three partner apps
+
the problem
Three partners, three engineering teams that don't report to me, and a regulator watching. Every extra surface in the spec was another legal review and another month.
what I chose
Ship portfolio tracking, not full onboarding. A read-only view of what a user already holds, and a reason to open it every morning.
what I gave up
Onboarding inside the partner app. Anyone who decided to invest had to leave and finish on ours. I traded conversion friction for a scope three legal teams would sign.
what happened
Partners shared no KYC data, legal cleared much faster, and the channel scaled to 17,000+ users tracking ₹7,000 Cr. The loops on top added +38% repeat engagement and +21% lead-to-call.
