Selected missions Work

Things I've shipped. On purpose.

I came to product through growth, so I still ask the boring question first: how does someone find this, and why would they come back?

01 Dezerv / Embedded tracker

Build on a road people already take.

Nobody wakes up wanting another wealth app. So instead of asking people to download one, we put a portfolio tracker inside Moneycontrol, Livemint and Inshorts. Where they already were, doing what they already did.

17,000+ users. 700 qualified leads a month. 75% lower CAC than paid channels.

Read the decisions

The choice. Version one tracked portfolios. That's it. No full onboarding, no grand tour. A smaller compliance surface, faster partner sign-off, and a reason to come back tomorrow.

My job. Sole PM across three external partner engineering teams, which is a polite way of saying three different release calendars. In 2025 I took full ownership of the embedded channel.

What made it stick. Daily portfolio movement, plus news tied to the holdings you actually own. Repeat engagement went up 38%. Lead scoring lifted lead-to-call by 21%.

The fine print. The product tracked ₹7,000 Cr in assets. The partner apps reach 20M+ people combined; that is their audience, not our user count. And tracked is not managed.

02 Wint Wealth / Investor tools

Answer the question. Then point at the product.

I owned the investor tools: the Bonds Directory, FD calculators, gold and silver rates. The order we built them in mattered as much as the traffic they brought.

7K → 153.4K monthly organic sessions, one year apart.

Read the decisions

The choice. Bonds Directory first. It connected what people were searching for with what Wint actually sold. Everything else queued behind it.

The machine. Five editors, seven subject-matter experts and a few agencies. Named authors, expert review and careful financial language, at 500+ articles a month. In this category, "roughly" is not a word.

The fine print. 7K to 153.4K a month, one year apart. Tools and content together, so not a controlled experiment proving one feature did it all. I won't pretend otherwise.

Field notes Working notes

Things I keep coming back to.

Three short notes from the work. Open one. It won't take long.

01Distribution is part of the product.

I learned this the expensive way, on my own stores. I could build a site and buy ads. I couldn't make that add up to a business. SEO changed the question to: where is someone already looking for this? Years later, the embedded tracker was the same question with more zeros.

02The smaller first version is often the better one.

At Dezerv, version one was portfolio tracking, before onboarding. Users got something useful. Partners got something small enough to approve. When several companies have to say yes before you ship, scope is the product decision.

03Good people don't always have the right CV.

When I built an SEO team, I went back to people I used to play games with and taught them from scratch. We became a team of seven. Sometimes you know what someone can do long before their CV has a word for it.

Player story About

I didn't take the straight line.

At BIT Mesra I was good at a video game and curious about how people made money online. That turned into seven online stores, a painful lesson in paid ads, and teaching myself SEO out of necessity.

One store started ranking. I cold-messaged an agency owner in Australia, became an intern, then an SEO team lead. Some of the people I trained were friends from those gaming nights.

Then Wint Wealth, then Dezerv. These days I work on financial products by day and build AI things by night. The route here was messy. Messy routes make better stories.
